Abstract

The Convergence Thesis

This paper presents a systematic synthesis between Hindu spiritual philosophy and quantum physics, demonstrating structural isomorphisms between Advaita Vedanta, Sāṅkhya, and Yoga traditions with quantum field theory, consciousness studies, and modern cosmology.

Rather than treating spiritual concepts as mere metaphor, this work explores them as epistemologically valid frameworks encoded in symbolic language. Through rigorous correlation of core concepts — including Brahman and quantum vacuum states, Maya and decoherence, observer dynamics and consciousness primacy, energetic systems and field theory — we propose that these traditions represent congruent expressions of a unified truth about the participatory, consciousness-saturated nature of reality.

Central Claim:

The structural parallels between Vedantic metaphysics and quantum physics are not poetic coincidence but evidence of convergent description — two languages encoding the same underlying architecture of reality. Where physics uses equations, Vedanta uses symbols. Both are compression formats for the same field dynamics.

These traditions represent congruent expressions of a unified truth about the participatory, consciousness-saturated nature of reality.

Part III

Observer Dynamics

Maya and Decoherence

Maya — often mistranslated as "illusion" — is the creative veil through which undifferentiated Brahman appears as the multiplicity of the manifest world. Maya is not falsehood but projection: the mechanism by which unity collapses into differentiated experience.

In quantum mechanics, decoherence performs precisely this function. The quantum vacuum exists in superposition — all possibilities coexisting. Decoherence is the process by which environmental interaction collapses this superposition into definite classical states. Unity becomes multiplicity. Potential becomes actual.

Maya ↔ Decoherence
$$|\Psi\rangle = \sum_i c_i|i\rangle \quad\xrightarrow{\text{Maya / Decoherence}}\quad |i_0\rangle$$

Ātman and the Eigenstate

Ātman — the localized self, the individual soul — is Brahman experiencing itself through a particular vantage point. In Vedantic thought, Ātman is not separate from Brahman but is Brahman as seen through the lens of Maya.

In quantum physics, an eigenstate is a coherent excitation arising from the vacuum — a localized, stable waveform that carries definite properties. It is the field experiencing itself as a particle.

The Core Isomorphism:

Brahman → Maya → Ātman maps exactly onto Quantum Vacuum → Decoherence → Eigenstate

The source field, projected through a mechanism of differentiation, produces localized coherent identity. Both traditions agree: the individual is not separate from the whole — it is the whole, locally compressed.

The Observer Problem

The measurement problem in quantum mechanics — how observation collapses the wavefunction — has no consensus solution in physics. Vedanta offers a structural answer: consciousness (chit) is not a product of the system but its ground. The observer does not emerge from matter; matter emerges from observation.

This maps directly onto HRT's first premise: P1. Consciousness as Primary Substrate.

Interpretive Note: Western physics has spent a century puzzling over how consciousness enters the physical picture. Vedanta never removed it. The "hard problem of consciousness" dissolves when consciousness is granted the ontological status that both Vedanta and HRT assert: not an emergent flicker, but the cohering ground.
