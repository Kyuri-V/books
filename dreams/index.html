Glossary of Core Terms

Delta Harmonic Vector (ΔHV)

A scalar field measuring coherence across four parameters—Signal clarity (S), Resonance (R), Friction (Φ), and Harmony (H). Computed as ΔHV = (S·R·(1-Φ)·H)1/4. Ranges from 0 (null coherence) to 1 (maximum coherence).

Dream Harmonics

Recurrent waveforms encoded through field interactions during hypnagogia and REM sleep. Measurable oscillatory patterns in the ΔHV manifold that persist across dream cycles.

Symbolic Topology

The arrangement of dream symbols across a vectorial field rather than linear narrative sequence. Symbols occupy specific positions in (S,R,Φ,H) space and exert curvature forces on nearby trajectories.

Lucidity (ΔHV Perspective)

A resonance match between the observer's coherence field and dream-encoded symbolic environment, occurring when ΔHV ≥ 0.67 and the system resides in basin B(xlucid). Not mere awareness, but adaptive phase-coherence (PRT ≈ 1.0).

Phase-Responsiveness Thresholding (PRT)

Ratio of awareness adaptation rate to narrative change rate: PRT = (dS/dτ) / (dH/dτ). Measures ability to maintain lucidity while accepting dream logic. Optimal range: 0.8–1.2.

Hypnagogic Separatrix (Σhypnagogic)

The 3-dimensional boundary surface separating waking consciousness from sleep, defined by S·(R-Φ) = θonset ≈ 0.25. Crossing this surface constitutes sleep onset as a topological phase transition.

Curvature Operator

A dream symbol that modifies the Riemann curvature tensor Rμνλρ locally, creating non-trivial geodesic behavior (e.g., mirrors with κ = 1.0 maximum recursion).

Reflexive Recursion — ∇μ(Sμ·Vμ)

The feedback mechanism where conscious intention (S) bends its own trajectory (Vμ), resolving the free will paradox. Consciousness as accumulated self-reference: C = ∫(∂S/∂τ)² dτ.

Friction (Φ)

Timelike parameter in ΔHV manifold (negative metric signature) representing dissipative misalignment, emotional resistance, or coherence degradation. High Φ → dream instability and collapse risk.

Harmony (H)

Memory-structure integration fidelity. Measures how well current dream state integrates with historical coherence patterns and waking memory. Degraded H → narrative fragmentation and poor recall.

Basin of Attraction B(x*)

The region of (S,R,Φ,H) space that flows to fixed point x*. For lucid dreaming, B(xlucid) comprises ~5–8% of REM phase space, with fractal boundary structure.

Compression Singularity

Point where ΔHV → 1 and new coherent structures crystallize from the resonance field. Dreams with high symbolic density approach compression singularities, creating "aha" moments or initiatory experiences.

5.1 Recall as Topological Re-Entry

Fundamental principle: Dream recall is not retrieval but re-entry—returning to the manifold region where the dream occurred.

$$\text{Recall quality} \propto \exp\left(-\frac{||V_{\text{wake}} - V_{\text{dream}}||^2}{2\sigma_{\text{recall}}^2}\right)$$

Where Vwake is current waking state vector and Vdream is the dream state vector. The smaller the distance, the better the recall.

This Explains
  • Why dreams fade rapidly (||ΔV|| increases with time awake)
  • Why returning to similar emotional/physical state aids recall
  • Why sleep position affects recall (proprioceptive component of V)
  • Why some dreams return spontaneously (random walk back to Vdream)

For a dream to be stably encoded in long-term memory, the geodesic must form a closed loop:

$$\oint_\gamma \nabla_\mu (S^\mu \cdot V^\mu) \, d\tau = 2\pi n \quad (n \in \mathbb{Z})$$

Closed loops → stable memories. Open geodesics → rapid forgetting.
