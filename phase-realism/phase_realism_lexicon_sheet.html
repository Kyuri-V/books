Primary Definitions
Field
The continuous generative substrate of reality. Not a container — the prior condition from which all organized expressions arise. Not derived.
Coherence
The field's intrinsic capacity for ordered relational organization, expressed dynamically through the primary causal dynamics of alignment, entrainment, and compression, and observable in the enduring patterns of stabilization and persistence. Ontologically a capacity; phenomenologically a process.
Phase
A regime of organized expression within the field. The field under a particular mode of self-organization. Phase boundaries are real transition zones.
Distinction
A real difference between two organized expressions of the field. Not projection, not error. The field's nature expressing itself as articulate form. Distinction is not the field's failure to remain one.
Relation
The mode by which distinct expressions are organized into coherent structure. Prior to substance. Relation preserves distinction while establishing connection — not fusion.
Stability
The resistance of a coherent phase-pattern to perturbation. Not permanence, not rigidity — the depth of a coherence well.
Persistence
The endurance of a stable pattern through time or transformation. Continuation of organizing coherence, not sameness of material.
Identity
The recognizability of a persistent organization across change. Downstream of persistence. Not a primitive — the observable face of coherence held through time.
Causal Grammar
Coupling
The general condition of causal-relational linkage between phase-organized structures. Umbrella term.
Alignment increase of structural compatibility within coupling
Entrainment synchronization of phase-patterns within coupling
Compression intensification of relational constraint toward reorganization
→ Phase transition threshold event of reorganization into a new regime
Critical Distinctions
Field ≠ Phase — the field is the substrate; a phase is a mode of organization within it
Phase ≠ Coherence — a phase is a regime; coherence is the capacity that makes phases possible
Coherence ≠ Stability — coherence is the ordering capacity; stability is resistance of a specific configuration
Stability ≠ Persistence — stability is structural resistance at a moment; persistence is continuation across time
Persistence ≠ Identity — persistence is endurance of pattern; identity is recognizability across change
Relation ≠ Fusion — relation preserves distinction while connecting; fusion erases the terms
Distinction ≠ Separation — distinction is real difference within the field; separation implies sealed-off independence
Signature Inversion

Difference is not the opposite of unity.
Difference is how unity becomes real.
