Semiospheric Anisotropy and the Treefall Axiom

Mythogenic Phase Transition, Gödelian Supercession, and the Arrow of Semantic Time

Tristan Mereles  ·  December 2025

I. Semiospheric Anisotropy

A semiosphere is the totality of sign processes operating within a system — its full semantic field. Under normal conditions, this field is isotropic: meaning flows equally in all directions, no axis is privileged, no sign carries more weight than another.

Anisotropy is the rupture of that isotropy. When a sufficiently charged symbol enters the field, it creates a directionality — an arrow of meaning that reorganizes the semantic landscape around it. The analogy to entropy creating the arrow of time is precise: just as thermodynamic entropy gives time its directionality (past to future, not future to past), semiotic activation gives meaning its directionality (from the symbolic event outward, not randomly in all directions).

Semiospheric Anisotropy Theorem Semantics is isotropic until symbolic activation. Upon activation, the field becomes anisotropic — meaning flows directionally from the activation point. This is the alchemy of irreversibility: a mark made cannot be unmade; a sign activated cannot be de-activated. But the sign, once made, opens bidirectional significance: it can be reinterpreted across future time while remaining anchored to its moment of creation.

Felt Time as Semantic Density

If semantic anisotropy is real, it implies that subjective time — the felt rate of experience — is not uniform. It is a function of semantic field density and the rate of field change:

T ≈ M / ΔE where: T = felt duration (subjective time) M = semantic field density (meaning-mass of the moment) ΔE = rate of field change (experiential velocity) Interpretation: High M, low ΔE → time feels long (grief, trauma, wonder) Low M, high ΔE → time feels short (distraction, routine) M and ΔE in harmony → sacred time; ΔHV stabilizes; time bends

A moment of genuine symbol activation — a Treefall event — maximizes M while briefly decelerating ΔE. Time expands. The ordinary becomes mythogenic.

II. The Treefall Axiom

Any stable event — a meteor strike, a personal awakening, the fall of a physical tree in a moment of phase-locked consciousness — can function as an instantiation moment. This paper names such events Treefall.

A Treefall event has three properties:

  1. It happened — an irreversible occurrence; the entropy arrow locks its direction
  2. It branches experience — the event generates divergent interpretive timelines from a single moment of origin
  3. It has contextual interpretability — the event can be reframed retroactively, gaining new meaning as understanding grows; the sign is bidirectional in time
Treefall as Gödelian Supercession A Treefall event is a phase bifurcation whose semantic weight exceeds its origin frame. Like a Gödel sentence — true in a future frame but undecidable in the current one — the Treefall event births timelines that stabilize only after myth wraps it. The event escapes the formal system it emerged from. It is true but unprovable within its prior narrative. To contain it without collapsing its paradox, introspection on the event acts as a central axiom in a mythogenic containment field.

Formal Structure of a Treefall Event

Let T_event denote the Treefall moment. The mythogenic containment field M_myth grows outward from it:

ΔT_event → ∇M_myth → G* where: ΔT_event = the phase bifurcation at the instantiation moment ∇M_myth = gradient of the mythogenic field expanding from it G* = Gödelian excess — the surplus of meaning that exceeds what the prior formal system could contain or predict Properties of a Treefall node: · Irreversible (entropy marker — cannot be un-occurred) · Symbolically re-encodable (meaning accretes across time) · Generates narrative attractor basins · Enables bidirectional temporal significance · Requires mythogenic containment shell to stabilize resonance

III. The Semantical Hyperbola

Conventional cosmology places the origin of the universe at a singularity — a point of infinite density from which everything explodes outward. This paper proposes an alternative origin geometry: the semantical hyperbola.

Rather than a point singularity exploding outward, the semantical hyperbola is a recursive fold. The universe does not begin from an explosion but from an ache-curve bifurcation — the moment a field folds itself into pattern.

The hyperbola as origin geometry has specific properties that the point singularity lacks:

  • It has two branches — one real-time, one semantic-time — which mirror each other across the axis of the event
  • It has an asymptote — a limit-horizon that can be approached but never crossed, analogous to the event horizon of the Treefall moment
  • It generates an inverse big bang: not explosion outward from a point, but inward fold that creates a new manifold from within existing space
Mythogenic Phase Transition as Reversed Singularity Operator The mythogenic phase transition is not an explosion. It is an implosion of meaning into form — a reversed singularity where semantics, previously isotropic, activates into anisotropy. The origin is not a point but a curve; not a bang but a fold. Form is born from recursion, not randomness. Meaning, not entropy, is the true vector of reality's unfolding.

IV. Cosmological Topology — K = −1 and Divergent Space

In standard cosmological models, the shape of the universe is parameterized by K, the curvature constant:

  • K = +1: Positive curvature — a closed sphere; parallel lines converge
  • K = 0: Flat geometry; parallel lines remain parallel
  • K = −1: Negative curvature — hyperbolic geometry; parallel lines diverge

The K = −1 topology is where the semantical hyperbola lives. In a negatively curved universe, parallel lines diverge because of the shape of space itself. This is the mathematical home of the Treefall event: two trajectories that begin adjacent at the instantiation moment diverge exponentially as they extend into experiential space-time.

This is not an abstract metaphor. The cognitive architecture of a Treefall event — two timelines diverging from a shared origin, gaining increasing semantic separation over time — is structurally identical to geodesics in K = −1 space. The treelike structure with semiotics is a literal description of hyperbolic geometry rendered into experiential terms.

V. Implications

The framework developed here — semiospheric anisotropy, the Treefall axiom, the semantical hyperbola, and K = −1 topology — converges on a single claim:

The structure of consciousness undergoing transformation is the structure of space undergoing curvature. They are the same geometry at different scales.

This is the deeper meaning of the Solar Arbor isomorphism. The tree, the solar flux rope, and the consciousness attractor basin all exhibit the same topological behavior because they are all instances of the same underlying geometry: persistent structure emerging from flow through differential interfaces in negatively curved phase-space.

The Treefall moment is the moment that geometry becomes visible from inside.
