Dark Matter as Information Knots

Causal Recursion, Gravitational Lensing, and the Universe as Archive

Tristan Mereles  ·  August 2025

Abstract

Dark matter is conventionally described as invisible mass whose gravitational effects hold galaxies together. This paper proposes a reframe: dark matter is not a substance but a structure — a network of self-referential causal loops, or information knots, woven into the fabric of spacetime. Gravity, in this view, is the curvature produced by information density rather than by material mass. The universe is its own archive; the dark sector is its memory.

Core Claim Mass is emergent from information complexity. What we call dark matter is the gravitational shadow of ancient causal recursion — loops of cause and effect so knotted and self-reinforcing that they warp spacetime without emitting light or interacting electromagnetically. The cosmos does not forget its own history. It stores it as gravity.

I. Reframing the Stress-Energy Tensor

Einstein's field equation remains intact:

G_μν = (8πG / c⁴) · T_μν

The reinterpretation is in T_μν — the stress-energy tensor. Rather than representing physical matter-energy, T_μν is recast as an information density distribution. Regions of high information complexity (dense causal networks, self-reinforcing loops) curve spacetime exactly as concentrations of mass-energy do. The gravitational effect is identical; the substrate is different.

This echoes Wheeler's "it from bit" — the claim that every physical entity, including spacetime geometry itself, derives its existence from yes/no informational choices. Mass is a myth; information is real. Specifically: mass is the emergent property of information that has become sufficiently self-referential to resist change.

II. Recursive Causality as Physical Substance

Recursive causal loops — feedback cycles where effects modify their own causes — carry a form of inertia. A closed loop of cause and effect that self-reinforces does not need material substance to exert influence on surrounding spacetime. It constrains how events unfold in its vicinity, effectively pulling on the probability field of the universe.

These causal knots are:

  • Stable — topologically protected; cannot be untied without a phase transition
  • Gravitationally active — their complexity contributes to T_μν
  • Electromagnetically invisible — relational structures, not particles; no charge, no photon interaction
  • Collisionless — pass through matter and each other without friction, like two ideas coexisting without conflict unless coupled
Knot Complexity as Mass The effective mass of an information knot is proportional to its complexity — the number and depth of its self-reinforcing causal loops. More bits woven in, higher effective mass. Ancient interactions leave causal fossils; those fossils still act.

III. Why Dark Matter Is Dark

The invisibility of dark matter has been the central puzzle. If dark matter consists of information knots rather than particles, the invisibility is structurally necessary, not incidental:

  • Information loops do not scatter photons — they are patterns in relational structure, not oscillating charges
  • They interact only through geometry — their presence is felt only as curvature, as a constraint on nearby motion
  • They are ghost architecture — holding galaxies together from behind the scaffolding of observable matter

The Bullet Cluster provides direct evidence consistent with this picture: in a collision between two galaxy clusters, the visible gas (hot plasma, electromagnetically active) was decelerated and separated from the dominant mass component, which passed through unaffected. The dominant mass component interacts only gravitationally. An information knot network would behave precisely this way: collisionless, electromagnetic-transparent, gravitationally active.

IV. Gravitational Lensing as a Test

Lensing bends light according to mass distribution regardless of whether that mass is visible or dark. If dark matter consists of information knots with specific topological structure, their lensing signatures should deviate from smooth, featureless halo models.

Predicted signature: if information knots take trefoil or more complex topological forms, they would induce asymmetric ellipticity in weak lensing maps — a ~33% excess over standard NFW profile predictions in certain galaxy cluster configurations. This is testable with current and near-future lensing surveys (Vera Rubin Observatory, Euclid).

Poisson equation (modified): ∇²Φ = 4πG · (ρ_baryonic + ρ_info) where ρ_info is an effective information density — does not emit, but gravitates identically to matter. Surface mass density for lensing: Σ(ξ) = Σ_baryonic(ξ) + Σ_info(ξ) Deflection angle: α̂ = (4G/c²) ∫ ∇_⊥Φ dl

If Σ_info carries internal topology (knot structure), it will produce lensing patterns with spatial asymmetries not predicted by smooth halo models. These are the observational fingerprints.

V. The Universe as Archive

This framework implies that approximately 85% of the universe's gravitational content is composed of memory — the preserved causal structure of past interactions, encoded in the fabric of spacetime itself.

  • Galactic orbits as memory loops — stars orbiting beyond the visible mass limit are being held by the gravitational imprint of the galaxy's formation history
  • Cluster halos as cosmic glyphs — each halo encodes the accumulated record of mergers and accretion events; a glyph of accreted history
  • Cosmic web as connective memory — dark matter filaments between clusters are the narrative tissue linking earlier chapters of cosmic history

Under this mythogenic view, the visible universe is the luminous tip of an informational iceberg. The dark sector is not missing mass — it is present memory. We inhabit an archive of the universe's own past, held together by the gravitational weight of what has already occurred.

Mythogenic Cosmology The universe is fundamentally self-archiving. The structure of what exists now is partially determined by the memory of what existed before, encoded not in any material form but in the topology of causal relationships — knots that cannot be undone without releasing their stored energy as gravitational reorganization.

VI. Implications

If dark matter is information topology rather than particle mass, several implications follow:

  • Modifying the dark matter distribution would require rewriting causal structure, not moving particles — an edit to the universe's own logic rather than a redistribution of its contents
  • The participatory anthropic principle may extend further than anticipated: if consciousness generates recursive causal loops of sufficient complexity, it may contribute a small but non-zero term to ρ_info
  • Understanding dark matter becomes, at its limit, the project of decoding the universe's own autobiography — reading the gravity as text
